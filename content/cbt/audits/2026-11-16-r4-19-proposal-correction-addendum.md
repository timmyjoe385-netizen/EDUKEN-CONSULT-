## 1. DUP-108 — Government: distinguish the Bureau from the Tribunal

### Issue in source draft
The source stem asks which body receives an allegation "for adjudication" and keys the Code of Conduct Tribunal. The explanation distinguishes the Bureau's administrative/compliance functions from the Tribunal's adjudication role, but the question should be precise about the constitutional process and avoid implying an unrestricted direct referral route.

### Safer proposed stem
**Which institution is responsible for adjudicating cases of alleged breach of the Code of Conduct for public officers?**

A. Code of Conduct Tribunal  
B. Public Complaints Commission  
C. Federal Character Commission  
D. National Judicial Council

**Proposed key:** A

**Proposed explanation:** The Code of Conduct Tribunal is the body that adjudicates cases of alleged breach of the Code of Conduct by public officers. The Code of Conduct Bureau performs related functions, including receiving and examining asset declarations and handling allegations in accordance with the constitutional procedure.

**QA note:** Confirm the precise constitutional wording and current JAMB Government syllabus depth before treating this as final. Do not state that every allegation is automatically referred directly to the Tribunal.

## 2. DUP-110 — Biology: remove the answer giveaway

### Issue in source draft
The original stem says: "An inhibitor that competes with a substrate for an enzyme's active site produces:" The stem repeats the defining phrase "competitive" and effectively supplies the answer.

### Proposed replacement stem
**When the concentration of substrate is increased sufficiently, what may happen to the effect of a competitive enzyme inhibitor?**

A. Its effect may be reduced because substrate molecules compete for the active site  
B. It permanently destroys every enzyme molecule  
C. It converts the substrate into an enzyme  
D. It prevents all enzyme activity regardless of substrate concentration

**Proposed key:** A

**Proposed explanation:** A competitive inhibitor and the substrate compete for the enzyme's active site. Increasing substrate concentration can increase the likelihood that substrate molecules bind to the active site, reducing the inhibitor's effect. The inhibitor does not necessarily destroy the enzyme.

**QA note:** Recheck that the distractors are plausible, parallel and unambiguous, and confirm that this level of enzyme inhibition is within the intended JAMB Biology syllabus coverage. This is a proposed revision, not a verified final item.

## 3. DUP-96 — Economics: make the next-best alternative explicit

### Issue in source draft
The source stem says a local council builds a market instead of repairing a rural road and asks what the road repairs represent. The explanation defines opportunity cost as the value of the next best alternative forgone. The stem can state more clearly that road repairs are the next-best alternative.

### Proposed replacement stem
**A local council has enough funds to undertake only one of two projects. It chooses to build a market, while repairing a rural road is its next-best alternative. The road repairs represent the:**

A. opportunity cost of the decision  
B. market price of the new market  
C. total revenue of the council  
D. law of diminishing returns

**Proposed key:** A

**Proposed explanation:** Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative forgone when a choice is made. Since the council chooses the market project instead of its next-best alternative, the road repairs represent the opportunity cost.

**QA note:** Confirm that the scenario is suitable for the intended Economics syllabus level and that the wording does not imply that every forgone option is the opportunity cost.
