- **Proposed topic:** Human physiology / Gas exchange in the lungs
- **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** The walls of alveoli are very thin and are surrounded by a dense network of blood capillaries. This arrangement mainly facilitates:

A. rapid exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide between air and blood  
B. the mechanical breakdown of food  
C. the filtration of urea from blood  
D. the production of red blood cells

**Correct option:** A

**Explanation:** Alveoli provide a large, thin, moist surface with a rich blood supply. These features allow oxygen to diffuse from the air in the alveoli into the blood and carbon dioxide to diffuse from the blood into the alveoli.

**Internal content checks**

- **Answer logic:** A is the only defensible answer. The other options describe digestive, excretory or blood-cell production processes, not the function of alveolar structure.
- **Concept distinction:** The held item tests airway protection during swallowing. This alternative tests the structural adaptation of alveoli for gaseous exchange.

That item concerns exchange of nutrients, gases and wastes between maternal and foetal blood; this draft concerns gas diffusion across the alveolar surface between air and pulmonary blood. No source-bank score reached 0.42.
- **Existing-proposal screen:** Highest score: **0.236**, against proposal DUP-74 (ultrafiltration). No proposal score reached 0.42.
- **Remaining caution:** A reviewer should confirm the expected level of detail on alveolar adaptations in the current UTME Biology syllabus and ensure the wording does not imply that alveoli alone perform every step of respiration.

## Batch 17 — DUP-224 (Biology)

- **Held source location:** batch 30, row 16
- **Retained comparison location:** batch 14, row 12
- **Held topic:** Plant biology / Water transport
- **Held source concept:** Xylem transports water and dissolved mineral ions from roots towards stems and leaves.
- **Draft ID:** R4-ADD-2026-11-20-DUP-224-B17
- **Proposed topic:** Plant ecology / Epiphytes
- **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** An orchid growing on the branch of a tree uses the tree only for physical support and obtains its own nutrients. The orchid is best described as:

A. an epiphyte  
B. a parasite  
C. a saprotroph  
D. a predator

**Correct option:** A

**Explanation:** An epiphyte grows on another plant for physical support but does not obtain its food from that host. Many orchids are epiphytes; they make their own food by photosynthesis and obtain water and mineral nutrients from sources such as rain and accumulated organic debris.

**Internal content checks**

- **Answer logic:** A is the only defensible answer. A parasite obtains resources from its host, a saprotroph obtains nutrients from dead organic matter, and a predator feeds on other organisms.
- **Concept distinction:** The held item tests water conduction through xylem. This alternative tests an ecological growth relationship in which one plant uses another for support without parasitising it.
- **Source-bank screen:** Highest combined stem-and-options character-trigram Dice score: **0.284**, against an ectoparasite item (batch 39, row 12). Both questions mention plant/organism relationships, but the concepts differ: an ectoparasite lives on a host and obtains resources from it, whereas an epiphyte uses another plant as support without taking nutrients from that plant. No source-bank score reached 0.42.
- **Existing-proposal screen:** Highest score: **0.208**, against proposal DUP-50 (Golgi apparatus and protein packaging). No proposal score reached 0.42.
- **Remaining caution:** Confirm that epiphytes and plant ecological relationships are within the current UTME Biology syllabus at the intended depth. Keep the distinction between epiphytism and parasitism explicit in teaching and review.
