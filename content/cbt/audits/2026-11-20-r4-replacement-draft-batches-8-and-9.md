## Batch 8 — DUP-41 (Biology)

- **Held source location:** batch 40, row 13
- **Retained comparison location:** batch 7, row 16
- **Held topic:** Evolution / Natural selection
- **Held source concept:** Natural selection favours heritable traits that improve reproductive success.
- **Draft ID:** R4-ADD-2026-11-20-DUP-41-B8
- **Proposed topic:** Evolution / Vestigial structures
- **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** The reduced pelvic bones found in some whales are examples of structures that provide evidence for evolution because they:

A. perform the same function as the whale's flippers  
B. are remnants of structures that were functional in ancestors  
C. show that whales are descended from modern fish  
D. develop only when an organism changes its habitat

**Correct option:** B

**Explanation:** Vestigial structures are reduced features that have lost all or most of their original function in an organism but were functional in its ancestors. Reduced pelvic bones in whales are an example and provide evidence of evolutionary change. They do not show that modern whales descended from modern fish.

**Internal content checks**

- **Answer logic:** B is the only defensible answer. A confuses vestigial structures with functional appendages; C makes an unsupported ancestry claim; D misstates how vestigial structures arise.
- **Concept distinction:** The held question tests natural selection and differential reproductive success. This alternative tests comparative anatomical evidence of evolution through vestigial structures.
- **Source-bank screen:** Highest combined stem-and-options character-trigram Dice score: **0.333**, against the forelimb bone-arrangement question (batch 23, row 13). That item concerns similarity in basic forelimb structure (homology); this draft concerns reduced ancestral structures. The related evolution topic warrants human contextual review even though the score is below 0.42.
- **Existing-proposal screen:** Highest score: **0.223**, against Biology proposal DUP-74 (ultrafiltration in the kidney). No proposal reached 0.42.
- **Remaining caution:** The specific whale example and the depth of vestigial-organ terminology should be checked against the current UTME Biology syllabus and teaching level. Keep draft-only pending owner review.

## Batch 9 — DUP-175 (Biology)

- **Held topic:** Classification / Vertebrates

- **Draft ID:** R4-ADD-2026-11-20-DUP-175-B9
- **Proposed topic:** Vertebrate adaptation / Bony fish
- **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** In many bony fish, the swim bladder is an adaptation that mainly enables the animal to:

A. maintain buoyancy at different depths  
B. pump water over the gills  
C. detect chemicals dissolved in water  
D. produce digestive enzymes

**Correct option:** A

**Explanation:** The swim bladder is a gas-filled organ in many bony fish. By adjusting the amount of gas in it, the fish can regulate buoyancy and remain at different depths without continuously swimming.

**Internal content checks**

- **Answer logic:** A is the only defensible answer. Gas exchange over the gills, chemical detection and enzyme production are not the primary functions of the swim bladder.
- **Concept distinction:** The held question tests a mammalian classification feature. This alternative tests a specialised adaptation in many bony fish.
