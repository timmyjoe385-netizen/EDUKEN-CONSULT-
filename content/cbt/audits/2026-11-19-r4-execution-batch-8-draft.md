## Replacement question

When a clean platinum wire is dipped into a solution of a metal salt and held in a non-luminous Bunsen flame, a lilac flame is observed. Which metal ion is most likely present?

A. Potassium ion (K⁺)  
B. Sodium ion (Na⁺)  
C. Calcium ion (Ca²⁺)  
D. Copper(II) ion (Cu²⁺)

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Potassium compounds characteristically produce a lilac flame in a flame test. Sodium gives a strong yellow flame, calcium gives a brick-red/orange-red flame, and copper(II) compounds commonly produce a blue-green flame.
