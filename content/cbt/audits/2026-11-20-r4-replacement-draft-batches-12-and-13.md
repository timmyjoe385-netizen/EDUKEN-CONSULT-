## Batch 12 — DUP-215 (Government)

- **Held topic:** Constitution / Amendment
- **Held source concept:** A formal change to the provisions of a constitution is a constitutional amendment.
- **Draft ID:** R4-ADD-2026-11-20-DUP-215-B12
- **Proposed topic:** Political participation / Public opinion
- **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** A government asks a representative sample of citizens to answer questions about a proposed education policy before making a decision. The findings are mainly used to assess:

A. public opinion  
B. judicial independence  
C. party discipline  
D. constitutional amendment

**Correct option:** A

**Explanation:** A representative survey gathers citizens' views on an issue and helps government assess public opinion. It does not determine court independence, enforce party discipline or amend the constitution.

**Internal content checks**

- **Answer logic:** A is the only defensible answer. The scenario explicitly describes collecting views from a representative sample; the other options name unrelated constitutional or institutional concepts.
- **Concept distinction:** The held question tests the meaning of constitutional amendment. This alternative tests the use of representative surveys to assess public opinion, a different Government learning outcome.
- **Source-bank screen:** Highest combined stem-and-options character-trigram Dice score: **0.375**, against an opposition-related item (batch 22, row 33). The overlap is chiefly the public/political context; the held replacement tests survey-based measurement of citizens' views rather than the role of opposition parties. No source-bank score reached 0.42.
- **Existing-proposal screen:** Highest score: **0.299**, against proposal DUP-77 (rule of law). No proposal score reached 0.42.
- **Remaining caution:** Confirm that public-opinion surveys and the stated use of representative samples are appropriate to the current UTME Government syllabus and expected level. A reviewer should also ensure the stem does not imply that surveys alone determine government policy.

## Batch 13 — DUP-108 (Government)

- **Held source location:** batch 22, row 35
- **Retained comparison location:** batch 4, row 33
- **Held topic:** Public administration / Accountability
- **Held source concept:** Public officials should explain and justify their use of public authority and resources.
- **Draft ID:** R4-ADD-2026-11-20-DUP-108-B13
- **Proposed topic:** Public administration / Ombudsman
- **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** A public complaints commission receives a citizen's complaint that a government department has treated the citizen unfairly and investigates the allegation. Which institution is performing this role?

A. the ombudsman  
B. the electoral commission  
C. the central bank  
D. the public service commission

**Correct option:** A

**Explanation:** An ombudsman investigates complaints of maladministration or unfair treatment by public bodies and may recommend corrective action. The other institutions have different responsibilities.

**Internal content checks**

- **Answer logic:** A is the only defensible answer. The scenario describes investigation of a complaint about maladministration; the electoral commission, central bank and public service commission have different mandates.
- **Concept distinction:** The held question tests answerability for public authority and resources. This alternative tests the ombudsman's complaint-investigation role; it does not restate the general meaning of accountability.
- **Source-bank screen:** Highest combined stem-and-options character-trigram Dice score: **0.389**, against an item on the National Judicial Council (batch 37, row 32). Both concern public institutions, but the tested functions differ. No source-bank score reached 0.42.
- **Existing-proposal screen:** Highest score: **0.335**, against proposal DUP-221 (caretaker government). No proposal score reached 0.42.
- **Remaining caution:** Confirm the treatment and preferred naming of the ombudsman/public complaints commission in the current UTME Government syllabus and Nigerian institutional context. The stem describes the general ombudsman function and does not claim that every commission has identical powers.
