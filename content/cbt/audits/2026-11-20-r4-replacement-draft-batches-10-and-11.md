## Batch 10 — DUP-99 (Literature in English)

- **Held source location:** batch 23, row 38
- **Retained comparison location:** batch 5, row 40
- **Held topic:** Literary criticism / Satire
- **Held source concept:** Satire uses humour, irony or ridicule to expose or criticise faults in people, institutions or social practices.
- **Draft ID:** R4-ADD-2026-11-20-DUP-99-B10
- **Proposed topic:** Prose fiction / Narrative technique
- **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** A novelist ends a chapter at the moment a character discovers an unexpected message, leaving its contents undisclosed until the next chapter. This technique is best described as:

A. a cliffhanger  
B. a denouement  
C. an exposition  
D. a flashback

**Correct option:** A

**Explanation:** A cliffhanger ends a section of a narrative at a suspenseful or unresolved moment, encouraging the reader to continue to discover what happens next. A denouement brings the main conflicts towards resolution; exposition supplies background information; a flashback presents an earlier event.

**Internal content checks**

- **Answer logic:** A is the only defensible answer. The scenario deliberately withholds the message's contents at a moment of suspense.
- **Concept distinction:** The held question tests satire as a mode of social criticism. This draft tests a narrative device used to sustain suspense; it does not redefine satire or test tone.
- **Source-bank screen:** Highest combined stem-and-options character-trigram Dice score: **0.333**, against the denouement question (held batch 25, row 37). The concepts are related to narrative progression but distinct: a cliffhanger suspends resolution, while denouement brings conflicts towards resolution. No source-bank score reached 0.42.
- **Existing-proposal screen:** Highest score: **0.330**, against proposal DUP-21 (flashback). The devices are distinct: a flashback moves narration to an earlier event, while a cliffhanger creates suspense through an unresolved moment. No proposal score reached 0.42.
- **Remaining caution:** Confirm that the term “cliffhanger” and its use as a narrative technique match the intended UTME Literature syllabus level. Retain draft-only pending independent subject review.

## Batch 11 — DUP-233 (Literature in English)

- **Held source location:** batch 26, row 37
- **Retained comparison location:** batch 16, row 38
- **Held topic:** Drama / Aside
- **Held source concept:** An aside is a remark heard by the audience but conventionally not by the other characters on stage.
- **Draft ID:** R4-ADD-2026-11-20-DUP-233-B11
- **Proposed topic:** Oral literature / Proverb
- **Difficulty:** Medium

**Question:** A character tells a younger sibling, “A stitch in time saves nine,” after the sibling ignores a small repair. The character uses the saying mainly to:

A. draw on traditional wisdom to advise that prompt action can prevent a bigger problem  
B. give performers instructions about movement and position on stage  
C. reveal private thoughts in a speech that other characters cannot hear  
D. establish a pattern of repeated sounds at the ends of poetic lines

**Correct option:** A

**Explanation:** “A stitch in time saves nine” is a proverb. It expresses the practical wisdom that dealing with a small problem promptly may prevent it from becoming a larger one. In dialogue, a proverb can communicate shared cultural knowledge or advice concisely.

**Internal content checks**

- **Answer logic:** A is the only defensible answer. B describes stage directions, C describes an aside or soliloquy-like device, and D refers to rhyme.
- **Concept distinction:** The held question tests the dramatic convention of an aside. This draft tests the meaning and communicative function of a proverb in dialogue; it does not test who can hear a stage speech.
- **Source-bank screen:** Highest combined stem-and-options character-trigram Dice score: **0.312**, against the held aside question (batch 26, row 37). The overlap is mainly the shared literary-dialogue context; the tested concept is different. No source-bank score reached 0.42.
- **Existing-proposal screen:** Highest score: **0.239**, against proposal DUP-85 (blocking in theatre). No proposal score reached 0.42.
- **Remaining caution:** Confirm the treatment of proverbs under the current Literature in English syllabus and check the contextual interpretation against the prescribed teaching level. Retain draft-only pending independent subject review.
