## Draft 1 — Government

- **Held target:** DUP-108 — batch 22 row 35
- **Held topic:** Public administration / Accountability
- **Held stem:** “Public accountability requires public officials to ___.”
- **Draft stem:** An allegation that a public officer has breached the Code of Conduct is referred for adjudication to the:
- **Options:**
  - A. Code of Conduct Tribunal
  - B. Public Complaints Commission
  - C. Federal Character Commission
  - D. National Judicial Council
- **Answer:** A
- **Explanation:** The Code of Conduct Tribunal adjudicates alleged breaches of the Code of Conduct for public officers. The Code of Conduct Bureau receives complaints and performs the related administrative and compliance functions.
- **Difficulty:** Medium
- **Syllabus fit:** Proposed under ethics and accountability in public office. The exact institution-level depth should be confirmed by a qualified Government reviewer.
- **Source check:** The Code of Conduct Tribunal's published service charter lists adjudication of breaches as a Tribunal function; the official Fifth Schedule assigns allegations of breach to the Code of Conduct Bureau.
- **Bank-wide lexical screen:** Highest similarity 0.371 against the 1,600 R4 source questions (batch 37 row 33); 0.375 against the 116 existing proposals (DUP-104). Both are below 0.42.
- **Review status:** Draft only; subject/syllabus review required.

## Draft 2 — Economics

- **Held target:** DUP-133 — batch 25 row 27
- **Held topic:** Market failure / Externalities
- **Held stem:** “A negative production externality exists when”
- **Draft stem:** In a simple two-sector circular flow model, the flow of factor services from households to firms is matched by a flow of:
- **Options:**
  - A. factor incomes from firms to households
  - B. taxes from households to government
  - C. imports from firms to foreign producers
  - D. subsidies from government to firms
- **Answer:** A
- **Explanation:** Households supply factors of production to firms, and firms pay factor incomes such as wages, rent, interest and profit to households in return.
- **Difficulty:** Medium
- **Syllabus fit:** Two- and three-sector circular-flow models are included in the JAMB Economics national-income syllabus.
- **Bank-wide lexical screen:** Highest similarity 0.378 against the 1,600 R4 source questions (batch 27 row 30, a question on a leakage from the circular flow); 0.282 against the 116 existing proposals (DUP-45). Both are below 0.42. The circular-flow topic overlap with the leakage item is related but tests a different flow; reviewer confirmation is still required.

## Draft 4 — Biology

- **Held target:** DUP-165 — batch 27 row 12
- **Held topic:** Transport / Blood clotting
- **Held stem:** “Which blood component is especially important in forming a platelet plug during blood clotting?”
- **Draft stem:** In the ABO blood group system, a person with blood group AB normally has which antibodies in the blood plasma?
- **Options:**
  - A. Anti-A only
  - B. Anti-B only
  - C. Both anti-A and anti-B
  - D. Neither anti-A nor anti-B
- **Answer:** D
- **Explanation:** A person with blood group AB has both A and B antigens on red blood cells and normally has neither anti-A nor anti-B antibodies in the plasma.
- **Difficulty:** Medium
- **Syllabus fit:** JAMB Biology includes blood groups and applying this knowledge to blood transfusion and paternity determination.
- **Bank-wide lexical screen:** Highest similarity 0.389 against the 1,600 R4 source questions (batch 18 row 14, which tests the allele combination for blood group O); 0.218 against the 116 existing proposals (DUP-38). Both are below 0.42. This is a related blood-group concept but tests plasma antibodies rather than genotype.
- **Review status:** Draft only; subject/syllabus review required.

## Draft 5 — Physics

- **Held target:** DUP-169 — batch 31 row 25
- **Held topic:** Mechanics / Weight
- **Held stem:** “Calculate the weight of a 5 kg object on Earth. Take g = 10 m/s².”
- **Draft stem:** The minimum speed a body must have at the Earth's surface to escape its gravitational field without further propulsion is called:
- **Options:**
  - A. terminal speed
  - B. orbital speed
  - C. escape velocity
  - D. angular velocity
- **Answer:** C
- **Explanation:** Escape velocity is the minimum initial speed required for an object to overcome Earth's gravitational attraction without further propulsion, neglecting air resistance and other bodies.
- **Difficulty:** Medium
- **Syllabus fit:** Escape velocity is explicitly included under the JAMB Physics gravitational-field topic.
- **Bank-wide lexical screen:** Highest similarity 0.329 against the 1,600 R4 source questions (batch 12 row 22); 0.205 against the 116 existing proposals (DUP-71). Both are below 0.42.
- **Review status:** Draft only; subject/syllabus review required.
